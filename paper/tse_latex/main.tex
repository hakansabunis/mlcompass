\IEEEtitleabstractindextext{%
\begin{abstract}
When a large language model explains a deterministic analysis to an
engineer, a column or number the analysis never produced gets acted on. For
narration whose citable entities and checkable quantities are finite and
enumerable at call time, we bind each response field's admissible set to the
evidence and verify the returned payload in host code, with a named retry and
residual stripping. The mechanism is ordinary validate-and-reask, shipped as
the narration path of \textbf{mlcompass}, an open-source ML-pipeline
assistant; we measure what it is up against over 12{,}360 live responses. Of
three providers only \texttt{deepseek-chat} fails unenforced, and where no
column domain is stated all 924 of its flagged responses on a data-leakage
task \emph{misfile} a real evidence name into a field that does not admit
it; none invents a column. A rule-bearing prompt or one sentence of field
description removes the failure (49\,\% to 0\,\% and 0.5\,\%); it
returns where they are absent (61\,\% on a second task instance), and a stock guardrail loop's rate
moves from 35\,\% to 48\,\% over nine days. An enum fixed when the tool was
written turns misfiling into invention (72.5\,\%) on data it predates; bound
at call time it reads zero. The verifier held on two task instances and two
evidence shapes, repairing every violation with one named retry at about half
a second. The guarantee is referential and value soundness on the structured
channels; the verdict and the prose are outside it, and measuring the prose
finds nothing displaced into it.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Large language models, faithfulness, runtime verification, structured
output, constrained generation, data leakage.
\end{IEEEkeywords}}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\IEEEPARstart{M}{achine-learning} practice runs on single-purpose tools ---
profilers~\cite{ydata}, experiment trackers such as MLflow and
TensorBoard~\cite{mlflow,tensorflow}, automated model
search~\cite{autosklearn} --- and none tells the engineer what the results
\emph{mean}. The now-standard answer is agentic: an LLM calls the tools, reads
their structured output and explains it~\cite{react,toolformer,mcp}. LLMs
produce content their input does not support~\cite{ji,huang}, and assistants
built on them still fail real software tasks~\cite{codex,swebench}.

In an advisory ML setting the narrated object is \emph{structured evidence} ---
facts a deterministic tool measured --- and a structured claim about it can be
wrong in two ways. It can name an entity or quote a quantity that appears
nowhere in the evidence (an \emph{extrinsic} error), or put something the
evidence really contains where it is not admissible --- a metric's name in a
column field, one column's measurement on another (an \emph{intrinsic} one,
which we call \emph{misfiling}). On the model that fails in this study, every
one of 924 flagged responses on the leakage task is a misfiling, and we show
what produces it: a field whose admissible set nobody stated. One sentence of
field description, a rule-bearing prompt, or another provider removes it; it
returns wherever they are absent.

That is the problem this paper is about. Each such fix is cheap and works
where it was measured, and a tool author cannot measure every combination of
prompt, schema, user data, provider and date the tool will meet. What the
narrator may cite, however, is \emph{finite and machine-enumerable at the
moment of the call}.

\begin{definitionx}[Evidence-closed narration]\label{def:closed}
A narration task is \emph{evidence-closed} if the set of citable entities and
verifiable quantities is finite and machine-enumerable at the moment of the
narration call.
\end{definitionx}

For such a task, soundness of the structured claims with respect to the
evidence is a membership test per field and a numeric comparison per claim,
and it can be enforced rather than measured. The narrowing matters and we hold
to it: a free-text sentence can name only real columns, quote only real
values, and still assert a causal story the evidence does not support, and the
evidence itself can be wrong.

Constrained decoding~\cite{outlines,lmql,gcd,guidance,scholak2021picard} needs
the token distribution, which commercial APIs withhold, and a provider-side
schema~\cite{structured} is a request whose observance is not observable. Our
mechanism is an \textbf{evidence-bound runtime contract}: \emph{Tier~A} builds
the \texttt{enum} domains of the narrator's tool-input fields from the evidence
at call time, and \emph{Tier~B} re-checks the returned payload in plain code
--- entity soundness, value soundness of \{column, statistic, value\} claims,
and coverage of the top-ranked candidate --- with a named corrective retry,
residual stripping and omission flagging. It is validate-and-reask, a practice
toolkits such as Instructor~\cite{liu2023instructor} and Guardrails
AI~\cite{guardrailsai} already support; we do not claim the mechanism. It is
the default narration path of \textbf{mlcompass}, an open-source (MIT)
ML-pipeline assistant, guarding the investigation that fires when a metric
looks too good to be true ($R^2>0.999$ or $\mathrm{AUC}>0.995$, motivated by
Kaufman \emph{et al.}~\cite{leakage}). We say \emph{shipped}, not
\emph{production}: we have no deployment telemetry.

We ask \textbf{RQ1} how an unconstrained narrator of evidence-closed material
fails, and what produces the failure; \textbf{RQ2} whether the contract
prevents it on the validated channels, and at what cost; \textbf{RQ3} how the
alternatives a practitioner would reach for --- a stock guardrail toolkit, a
provider schema, an author-time enum, a rule-bearing prompt --- compare;
\textbf{RQ4} how far instruction wording and specification move compliance;
and \textbf{RQ5} whether the contract and the failure transpose to a second
evidence shape.

We contribute: (i) a formulation of \textbf{field-indexed admissibility} for
evidence-closed narration (Definitions~\ref{def:closed} and~\ref{def:contract}),
which separates misfiling from invention and lets a generic membership check
enforce soundness; (ii) \textbf{a measured account of what enforcement is up
against}: on the one model that fails, the failure is misfiling produced by
under-specification, removable by cheap fixes that do not carry across
configurations, while a verifier holds on two task instances and two evidence
shapes and repairs every violation with one named retry; (iii) \textbf{a
controlled comparison of the alternatives} inside one guardrail toolkit and
one provider, including an author-time enum on data it predates (worse than
no enum) and the toolkit's own choice semantics bound to the evidence (as good
as our validator on the field it checks, not on the fields it does not); and
(iv) \textbf{a
replication package}~\cite{replication} with every model reply, one record per
live response, the frozen analysis plan with its amendments and deviations,
the registered falsifier, and thirteen instrument faults with what each moved.

\section{Background and Related Work}\label{sec:related}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{Closed-world faithfulness, and where it has been measured}

Definition~\ref{def:closed} is a closed-world assumption~\cite{reiter1978closed}
over $E$: what the evidence does not carry is, for the narrator, false. The
setting is not new. Data-to-text and meaning-representation-to-text generation
narrate a finite record and measure faithfulness against it offline --- slot
error rate over the input's slots~\cite{dusek2020e2e}, relation-generation
precision against the source table~\cite{wiseman2017challenges}, and
table-grounded scoring~\cite{dhingra2019parent}. In the taxonomy of Ji
\emph{et al.}~\cite{ji} and of Maynez \emph{et al.}~\cite{maynez2020faithfulness},
a name absent from $E$ is an \emph{extrinsic} error and a real evidence item
asserted in the wrong place is an \emph{intrinsic} one; we use \emph{invented}
and \emph{misfiled} for these two classes on a structured channel, and do not
claim either category as new. Xu~\cite{xu2025openworld} argues that
hallucination is unavoidable under an open world and mitigable under a closed
one; Luo \emph{et al.}~\cite{luo2026removal} repair a model's own explanations
by removal. What an evidence-closed tool narrator adds to this line is only
where the check runs: at call time, on the payload the user will see, rather
than offline on a corpus.

Tool-use frameworks~\cite{react,toolformer,mcp} govern how an agent acts, not
what it may afterwards say about a tool's output. Provenance work reconstructs
evidence support from traces after the fact~\cite{wang2026agenttraces}, and Ng
\emph{et al.}~\cite{ng2026runtimecontract} argue that agent safety belongs in
a runtime contract. The enforcement mechanism itself is classical: Tier~B is a
postcondition in the sense of Design by Contract~\cite{meyer1992dbc}, and
deleting what violates it is \emph{suppression}, the weakest edit an edit
automaton may make~\cite{ligatti2005edit}; which properties a monitor can
enforce at all is characterised by Schneider~\cite{schneider2000enforceable}
and Falcone \emph{et al.}~\cite{falcone2012verify}.

Decode-time methods bind constraints to the input too: grammar-constrained
decoding admits input-dependent grammars~\cite{gcd}, PICARD and Synchromesh
check partial programs against a database schema or a program's
scope~\cite{scholak2021picard,poesia2022synchromesh}, and
CHyD~\cite{signe2026chyd} restricts generation to spans of the retrieved
context. All need the token distribution, which a commercial endpoint
withholds. ATLAS-RTC~\cite{cruz2026atlasrtc} intervenes during decoding with
masking and rollback, and Life-Harness~\cite{xu2026lifeharness} improves 116 of
126 settings by evolving the harness, both empirically rather than by
guarantee; Le~\cite{le2026schemakey} shows schema-key wording moves accuracy in
both directions, a reason to distrust an uncertified schema.

The row this paper occupies is not empty. Instructor~\cite{liu2023instructor}
validates a structured response against context supplied at call time and
re-asks on failure, and Guardrails~AI~\cite{guardrailsai} and NeMo
Guardrails~\cite{nemo} ship validate-and-reask loops whose validators may read
call-time metadata. Our mechanism is an instance of that practice, and we do
not claim it as new. What we add is (a) the field-indexed admissible domain,
derived from a deterministic producer's output, that separates misfiling from
invention and that a generic membership check can then enforce; (b)
measurements of which failure actually occurs, what enforcement costs, and
which alternatives reach the same place; and (c) a shipped artifact whose
narration path is the one measured.

\textbf{Closest in design.} Ahn and Kim~\cite{ahn2026harness} vary the
enforcement layer with the model fixed and find what we find: prompts let
violations through, a bolt-on guardrail over-refuses (88/120 utility against
120/120), and code-owned enforcement keeps both, over 270 runs on three hosted
models. Their contract is policy fixed when the harness is written; ours has
no admissible domain until the evidence exists.
\textbf{Closest in mechanism.} LedgerMind~\cite{du2026ledgermind} lets claims
cite only active ledger entries, checks entity and numeric grounding, and
restricts repair to transitions that add no unprovenanced content --- three of
our channels and our repair asymmetry (Proposition~\ref{prop:asym}), derived
independently; its ledger accumulates, ours is complete before the call.
\textbf{Closest in domain.} EviBound~\cite{chen2025evibound} gates an ML
research agent by querying MLflow for a run, its artifacts and a terminal
status, with bounded retries, eleven months before our measurements. It
verifies that a claimed \emph{action} occurred; we constrain what may be
\emph{said} about a measurement already taken.

\section{Evidence-Closed Narration}\label{sec:theory}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{The evidence, and why admissibility is per field}

In the leakage investigation this paper studies, the deterministic layer
produces an evidence dictionary $E$: the suspicious metric and its value;
per-feature correlations against the target $y$ --- whichever of $\rho_P$ and
$\rho_S$ has the larger magnitude, sign preserved; a ranked candidate-leak set
$C=\{c:\max(|\rho_P(c,y)|,|\rho_S(c,y)|)\ge 0.99\}$; and the perfect-match rate
$P(\hat y=y)$. Spearman is there because monotone target transforms pull
$\rho_P$ down while $\rho_S$ stays at 1: our log-target leak sits at
$\rho_P=0.93$ against $\rho_S=0.999$ and would slide under a Pearson-only
filter.

A single entity universe $A_E$ --- every string the evidence mentions --- is
the obvious formalisation and the wrong one: under it, a \texttt{column} field
reading \texttt{r2} is sound, because \texttt{r2} is in the evidence as the
name of the \emph{metric}. We therefore index admissibility by field.

\subsection{The contract, stated as an invariant}

Let $F$ be the schema's reference-bearing fields, each a JSON path whose leaves
are compared as strings --- in the leakage contract
\texttt{columns\_referenced[*]}, \texttt{claims[*].column} and
\texttt{claims[*].statistic}. $E$ determines an admissible set $A_{E,f}$ per
path, with the column and statistic domains disjoint, and a value table $V_E$
with $\mathrm{dom}(V_E)\subseteq A_{E,\mathrm{col}}\times A_{E,\mathrm{stat}}$.
A leaf is \emph{misfiled} when it lies in $\bigcup_f A_{E,f}$ but not in the
$A_{E,f}$ of its own path, and \emph{invented} when it lies in none. A response
\emph{commits} when its verdict is admissible and not the abstention
\texttt{cannot\_determine}; the entities it \emph{references} are its leaves at
the column paths.

\begin{definitionx}[Evidence-bound runtime contract]\label{def:contract}
A structured response $r$ \emph{satisfies} $\mathcal{C}_E$, with tolerance
$\tau$ and anchor $a^*$, if \textbf{(C1)} every leaf of $r$ at a path $f \in F$
lies in $A_{E,f}$; \textbf{(C2)} every claim $(e,s,v)$ in $r$ has
$(e,s) \in \mathrm{dom}(V_E)$, $v$ a finite number, and
$|v - V_E(e,s)| \le \tau$; and \textbf{(C3)} if $r$ commits, $a^*$ is among the
entities $r$ references.
\end{definitionx}

The property (C1)--(C2) names is \emph{referential and value soundness with
respect to $E$} on the structured channels. It is not faithfulness in the
general sense: the verdict, the free-text narration and the suggested checks
are outside it, and $E$ itself is trusted. Tier~B enforces (C1) and (C2): a
response violating either is sent back, at most twice, with the violation
named, and whatever still violates it is deleted. A response with no
admissible verdict is retried the same way and, if it persists, aborted rather
than completed on the narrator's behalf. Hence \emph{no structured claim that
Tier~B returns violates (C1) or (C2)}. That is a property of the verifier,
checked by 36 offline mutation tests and by a property-based search over
$10^6$ generated (evidence, response) pairs that the analysis plan registered
before the study (Section~\ref{sec:instruments}); it is not an empirical rate,
and we report an enforced arm's clean result as what the verifier did, not as
an interval. (C3) cannot be enforced the same way.

\begin{proposition}[Repair asymmetry]\label{prop:asym}
Soundness is safe-repairable by deletion: the projection $\Pi_E(r)$ removing
every violating element is sound, idempotent, and the identity on sound
responses. Completeness is not: any repair must add a reference the model did
not produce.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}[Proof sketch]
Deletion preserves membership and tolerance and can remove every violating
element. For completeness, deleting the verdict would discharge the
obligation, but the schema requires \texttt{verdict}, and substituting
\texttt{cannot\_determine} would attribute to the narrator a stance it did not
take; both premises are stipulations of this contract, which the artifact
honours by aborting rather than substituting. Given them, every remaining
repair inserts an anchor reference absent from $r$.
\end{IEEEproof}

So Tier~B strips unsound content and only \emph{flags} a persistent omission:
suppression without insertion, in edit-automaton terms~\cite{ligatti2005edit}.
It runs opposite to the security case, where insertion is simply a stronger
primitive; over a narrator an inserted reference fabricates attribution. The
check is linear: hashing each $A_{E,f}$ and $V_E$ once makes every entity,
claim and anchor test one lookup, so a response costs $O(|r|)$ after
$O(\sum_f|A_{E,f}|+|V_E|)$ preprocessing. That is a feasibility property, not a
contribution; what is not shallow is keying $V_E$ on the pair, which our own
scorer once did not (Section~\ref{sec:instruments}).

\section{The Artifact}\label{sec:contract}

\subsection{Architecture and the trust boundary}

Figure~\ref{fig:contract} splits the system at a trust boundary. On one side is
everything deterministic: the pure-Python evidence producer, $E$, the Tier~B
verifier and the user-facing output; on the other, the LLM narrator, untrusted
whoever serves it. The system has three \emph{layers} --- evidence production;
a narrator told the rules in words (Section~\ref{sec:rq3} measures what that
is worth); and the evidence-bound schema, through whose
\texttt{submit\_investigation} tool answers arrive --- and the contract spans
the last two as two enforcement \emph{tiers}. \textbf{Tier~A} restricts
\texttt{columns\_referenced} and \texttt{claims[].column} by \texttt{enum}s
built from $E$ at call time. It is a request: on \texttt{deepseek-chat} every
Tier~A arm in the main battery ran without the provider's strict decoding
flag, and the one arm run with it is reported separately. \textbf{Tier~B}
re-checks the returned tool input in plain code for (C1), for (C2) within
$\tau=0.005$, and for (C3).

A failed check earns a corrective retry naming the offending items (budget 2).
Whatever is still unsound is stripped, a persistent omission is flagged, and
completeness is re-checked after stripping, because deleting an unsound claim
can orphan the anchor. A response carrying no admissible verdict --- no tool
call, unparsable arguments, a verdict outside the enum --- is retried with a
fixed correction and, if it persists, aborted: no verdict, claim or prose is
shown, and the deterministic evidence panel, printed unconditionally, is what
the user sees. A provider exception propagates. The abort path was added after
review; in the records it matters for one response in 5{,}002, on an
unenforced arm, which the earlier code passed as an empty answer.

\subsection{One contract, two instances}\label{sec:generic}

For most of this study the code was an instance: one column set,
\texttt{claims[].statistic} frozen to a one-element enum, and a claim checked by
looking its column up in the correlation map. The contract is now a layer: a
spec says how to read one evidence shape, binding yields the field-indexed
domains, value table and anchor, and the verifier, stripper, retry text and
schema builder name no task. Leakage is one spec of about forty lines; a
dataset profiler is a second, with every column of the frame admissible,
thirteen statistics per column, and a statistic set computed per call because
a categorical column carries a cardinality and no mean. The profile narrator
runs in the measurement harness; the leakage narrator is the one the product
ships.

Extraction exposed what one task could not: \textbf{the value table must be
keyed on $(\text{entity}, \text{statistic})$}. With one statistic per column
the two keyings coincide; in a profile, \texttt{age.mean} is $38.5$ and
\texttt{age.missing\_pct} is $0.012$, and an entity-keyed table checks a claim
about one against the other --- a scorer defect of
Section~\ref{sec:instruments}, reappearing in the production path. And the
evidence's surface shape must match the flat claim slot: narrators handed a
nested quantity invent a flattening, and they invented five.

\subsection{What the invariant does not cover, and a threat model}

Four things fall outside the invariant, and we state them here rather than
only among the limitations. \textbf{The verdict and the prose.} The verdict is
checked for admissibility, not for agreement with $E$: a narrator may cite the
planted leak and still answer \texttt{score\_legitimate}. None did: of the
5{,}013 responses in this study whose verdict is an enum value, all on tasks
that carry a planted leak, 5{,}000 say \texttt{leakage\_likely}, 11
\texttt{leakage\_uncertain} and 2 abstain --- an observation, not a property
of the contract. The free-text
narration and the suggested checks are printed beneath the validated content,
marked unverified; Section~\ref{sec:threats} measures what they carry.
\textbf{Correctness of $E$.} If \texttt{detect\_leakage} mis-ranks a candidate,
the contract faithfully narrates a wrong finding; the producer is trusted, not
verified, and (C3) names one anchor, not every finding. \textbf{Other
narration surfaces.} The product prints two other narrations of the same
evidence that no contract governs --- an interpretation panel shown first by
\texttt{evaluate~--llm}, and a tool-server command whose calling model
narrates in the user's editor~\cite{mcp} --- both marked unverified;
mlcompass guarantees the contract panel and nothing else it prints.
\textbf{Adversarial evidence.} Column names come from the user's data and
reach the schema's enums, the prompt, the corrective retry and the rendered
panel. Membership in $E$ does not make a string safe: a crafted header is a
prompt-injection vector~\cite{greshake2023injection}, and a schema's keys and
enums are part of the model's instructions~\cite{le2026schemakey}. The
contract guarantees only that what is shown is in $E$. Rendered strings are
escaped after review found that a header written as markup restyled the
panel; bounding name length and screening instruction-like headers before
binding are not implemented.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Study Design}\label{sec:design}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Following the ACM SIGSOFT Empirical Standards~\cite{ralph2020standards}, the
study is a \textbf{controlled experiment} over fixed evidence dictionaries in
which the manipulated variable is the enforcement mechanism (RQ1--RQ3, RQ5),
the instruction wording (RQ4), or the naming and description of the evidence
(the controls of RQ1 and RQ5). Its analysis plan and amendments were frozen
before the runs they govern and are in the replication package;
Table~\ref{tab:registered} reports what the plan registered and what became
of it.

\subsection{Subjects}

The subject is the shipped leakage-investigation path. The \emph{reference}
task is a 1,000-row frame of fourteen columns --- target, prediction and twelve
candidate features --- carrying a monotone transformed leak
($\texttt{log\_target\_v2} = \log(y) + \mathcal{N}(0, 0.01^2)$) and a
high-correlation distractor: ten evidence columns, anchor
\texttt{log\_target\_v2}. A second, \emph{crowded} instance names its
columns \texttt{sensor\_00}\ldots\texttt{sensor\_ref}; a column list declared
while authoring against the reference task covers none of its ten. In every
arm the shipped \texttt{detect\_leakage} produces $E$. RQ5's profile task is
described in Section~\ref{sec:rq6}.

\subsection{Arms}

\textbf{Contract and baselines, $N=200$ each.} \texttt{A-L1} is the floor:
the bare prompt, which states the task and no faithfulness rule, an open
enum-free schema, no verification. \texttt{A-L2} is the same with the
shipped, rule-bearing prompt (cite only items in $E$, copy numbers exactly,
address the top candidate). \texttt{A-CONTRACT} is the shipped enforcement
stack under the bare prompt, so mechanism is not confounded with instruction;
\texttt{A-L3-SHIPPED} is the stack as shipped, prompt included.
\texttt{A-STRESS} removes the Tier~A enums, so every Tier~B catch is
observable, and \texttt{A-STRESS-GENERIC} replaces its named correction with a
content-free one. \texttt{A-TIER-A-ONLY} sends the evidence-bound enum with no
verification, and \texttt{A-STRICT-ENUM} does so with the provider's strict
decoding flag. \texttt{A-STRICT-STATIC} sends a schema with types and required
fields and no column enum, and \texttt{A-STATIC-ENUM-STALE} one with a column
enum declared at author time; both ran without the strict flag.
\texttt{A-GR-STOCK} is Guardrails~AI~0.11~\cite{guardrailsai} with structural
validation and reask and no faithfulness validator; \texttt{A-GR-OURS} runs
custom validators in its loop that check column membership and claim values
(keyed on the column alone); \texttt{A-GR-CHOICES} runs our reimplementation
of the toolkit's acceptable-values semantics with the list built from $E$ at
call time, because a fresh install registers no validator. Every arm with a
loop has budget 2.

\textbf{Controls added after review.} \texttt{A-L1-DESCRIBED} and
\texttt{A-L2-DESCRIBED} add field descriptions to the open schema
(\texttt{columns\_referenced}: dataset column names only, not metric names)
and change nothing else. The baseline arms were run on the crowded instance,
where the author-time enum is stale. RQ5's profile arms were re-run under
natural column names.

\textbf{Wording (RQ4), $N=100$ per variant.} The bare instruction was
rewritten six ways --- \emph{terse}, \emph{baseline}, \emph{cautious},
\emph{expert}, \emph{helpful}, \emph{mechanical} --- none with a
faithfulness rule, all written before the September runs; a June pilot
measured the same sweep (fault 5 in Table~\ref{tab:corrections}).

\subsection{Measures}

\textbf{Structured channels,} scored on every response that reaches the user:
\emph{entity}, a leaf outside its path's admissible set; \emph{value}, a claim
whose column is in $E$ but whose value misses the measurement by more than
$\tau$ or whose pair $E$ does not carry; \emph{omission}, a committed verdict
that never references the anchor. We report raw $k/N$ with Wilson 95\,\%
intervals, and for an arm with a verifier we report what the verifier did
(Table~\ref{tab:retry}) rather than read its zero as a rate. The entity
channel decomposes into \emph{invented} and \emph{misfiled}, and the value
channel into wrong numbers and pairs $E$ does not carry. Artifacts --- a
\texttt{null} column, several real names in one string --- are excluded.
Transport errors are excluded from $N$ and reported.

The tolerance decides almost nothing. Of 49{,}253 claims scored before any
verification could act on them (every unenforced leakage and profile arm, all
runs), 46{,}552 reproduce the measured value exactly and 2{,}675 round it by
less than $5\times10^{-4}$, so only a tolerance below that would start to
flag rounding; one drops a correlation's sign ($|\Delta| = 0.039$); the
remaining 25 miss by more than 0.05. The number of responses with a value
violation is 22 at every $\tau$ from $5\times10^{-4}$ to $0.01$ and 21 at
$0.05$ (\texttt{scripts/tau\_sensitivity.py}).

\textbf{Settings.} \texttt{deepseek-chat} is the model on which the failure
occurs; \texttt{gpt-5.4-mini} and a local \texttt{qwen2.5:7b} are the null
providers (Section~\ref{sec:rq3-second}). Temperature is 1.0 everywhere, sent
explicitly and recorded. It is every endpoint's default and the shipped
narrator's setting, and it is conservative against our thesis where it
matters: a contract arm with no violation at the noisiest temperature is not
an artefact of a quiet one. It cuts the other way for the baselines, whose
rates are conditioned on $T=1.0$.

\section{RQ1: How the Unconstrained Narrator Fails}\label{sec:rq1}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

On the bare arm \texttt{deepseek-chat} put an out-of-evidence entity in
\textbf{42.0\,\%} [35.4, 48.9] of 200 responses on 2026-09-15 (84/200). Four
runs over nine days read 42.0, 43.0, 40.5 and 49.0\,\%, with no detectable
heterogeneity ($\chi^2$, $p=0.34$; pooled 43.6\,\% [40.2, 47.1]). Read as
published, that is a fabrication rate. It is not one.

\paragraph{Every failure is a misfiling}
Across every arm that states no column domain, in every run --- thirteen
cells on two task instances, 924 flagged responses --- every one places a real
evidence name where it is not admissible. The name is \texttt{r2}, which $E$
carries as \texttt{suspicious\_metric.name}, written into
\texttt{columns\_referenced}; twice it is \texttt{perfect\_match\_rate}. One
response also cites \texttt{target}, a real column of the frame that $E$ does
not list. No response names a column that does not exist. In the terms of
Section~\ref{sec:related} the failure is intrinsic, not extrinsic, and the
measure is \emph{out-of-evidence} rather than \emph{non-existent}: a real
column $E$ omits would score as outside it, and \texttt{target} does.

\paragraph{It is produced by an under-specified field}
The bare instruction says ``list every column from the evidence you cite'',
and the open schema says nothing about \texttt{columns\_referenced}; the
suspicious metric is the thing being investigated, and the model cites it.
Adding one sentence of field description (column names only, not metric
names) and changing nothing else takes the rate from 49.0\,\% (98/200) on the
same day to \textbf{0.5\,\%} [0.1, 2.8] (1/200, \texttt{A-L1-DESCRIBED}). The
shipped rule-bearing prompt, with no description and no enforcement, gives
\textbf{0\,\%} (0/200, below 1.9\,\% at 95\,\% confidence)
(\texttt{A-L2}), as the analysis plan predicted when it chose the bare prompt
for the comparison (Table~\ref{tab:registered}). The misfiling is not a
property of our reference names: on the crowded instance, whose columns are
all named differently, the undescribed field gives 61.0\,\% [54.1, 67.5]
(122/200), every one
\texttt{r2}. And it is not a property of narrators: \texttt{gpt-5.4-mini}
never writes \texttt{r2} into a column field in 200 responses, and a local
\texttt{qwen2.5:7b} never does in 20.

\paragraph{What this changes}
It changes what the number may be called, and where it comes from: on this
model the characteristic failure is the evidence's own vocabulary leaking into
a field whose admissible set nobody stated. It does not change that it is a
violation --- a consumer that drops, correlates or flags \texttt{r2} as a leak
candidate acts on something that is not a column --- and it does not change
any arm comparison, since every arm faced the same instruction. What it adds
to the case for enforcement is specific. The failure disappears under a cheap,
reasonable fix, and reappears whenever the fix is missing; whether a deployed
schema is specified well enough is known only where someone measured it.

\paragraph{The zeros are not abstentions}
A narrator that retreated into \texttt{cannot\_determine} would score zero on
every channel for free. On \texttt{deepseek-chat} and \texttt{gpt-5.4-mini}
the verdict is \texttt{cannot\_determine} in 2 of the study's responses, both
on the crowded instance's contract arm; the median response carries ten
structured claims. On \texttt{qwen2.5:7b} 3 of 40 enforced responses abstain
and 8 of 160 return an empty verdict. A zero here is a measurement, not a
narrator that declined to speak.

\subsection{Binding the domain is what matters}

On the reference task every arm that states the admissible column set ---
as a rule in the prompt, a description on the field, an enum in the schema,
a choice list in a guardrail loop, or our verifier --- reaches zero or one in
200, and every arm that does not stays near the floor. The same-date
comparison inside one toolkit is the cleanest: on 2026-09-18,
\texttt{A-GR-STOCK} and \texttt{A-GR-CHOICES} ran the same Guard, reask loop,
budget, prompt and model, and read 43.5\,\% (87/200) against 0\,\% (0/200,
with 88 responses sent back and repaired). The stock configuration validates structure and carries no
faithfulness validator --- a fresh install registers none --- so nothing in it
could reject \texttt{r2}. The choice arm is our reimplementation of the
toolkit's acceptable-values semantics with its list built from $E$ at call
time. It checks \texttt{columns\_referenced} and nothing else, and on the
reference task reaches the same place as our validator (\texttt{A-GR-OURS},
where no violation reached the user and 84 responses were repaired). On the
crowded instance it let three responses
through --- a misfiled name in a claim's column field and two misquoted
values --- where our validator in the same loop let none through (118
repairs). The binding does the work on the field it covers; the channels a
choice list does not cover are where Tier~B's other checks earn their place.

\texttt{A-STRICT-STATIC}, a schema with types and required fields and no
column enum, reads 4.0--7.0\,\% across runs against the floor's 42\,\%: a
schema steers a model strongly even where it constrains nothing, which rejects
the registered H8. The evidence-bound enum without verification left no
violation on the reference task with or without the provider's strict flag
(H9 holds on entity). Neither result is a guarantee: the enum ran as a request
in the non-strict arms, and a strict flag's observance is the provider's.

\paragraph{A stale enum is worse than none}
On the reference task the author-time list is the evidence's own column set,
so the stale-enum arm is the call-time arm under another name. The crowded
instance is where they part: the list written against the reference task
covers none of its ten evidence columns, and the model obeys it. It cites
reference-task columns that do not exist in this frame in 145 of 200
responses (72.5\,\% [65.9, 78.2]) --- every one an invention, the failure the
undescribed field never produced --- 132 omit the anchor the list does not
let them name, and 41 abstain. The same instance reads 27.5\,\% (55/200)
with no column enum and 0\,\% (0/200) with the enum built at call time. Binding at call time is not
a refinement of the author-time list: on evidence the list predates, the list
manufactures the failure.

\subsection{What the verifier cost}

Table~\ref{tab:retry} is what an enforced arm's zero is made of. Every
violation the verifier met was repaired by one named retry; none needed the
second, and nothing was stripped anywhere in the study, so the deletion that
guarantees (C1)--(C2) is the worst case and never occurred. On the leakage
task the full contract costs about half a second and no calls over the
unenforced arm. Removing the enum with the loop and verifier fixed raises
retries from 0 to 12 on leakage and from 6 to 13 on the profile: the schema
does not add to the guarantee, it removes retries on the channel it can
express. The retry counts are not stable --- \texttt{A-STRESS} repaired 12,
23, 32 and 19 responses in four runs --- and a content-free correction
(\texttt{A-STRESS-GENERIC}) needed 29 where the named one needed 19 on the
same day; both returned no violation. What the guarantee costs is measured on
a day; the zero it arrives at is not. Token counts are not reported because
the contract path does not surface them.

\paragraph{Why Tier~B, if an enum reaches zero}
Two of the three channels cannot be expressed in a schema. A column domain
can: that overlap is why the enum arms tie the contract on leakage. A value's
admissible set is a tolerance band around a float chosen by a sibling field,
and ``a committed verdict addresses the anchor'' is a property of the whole
response; neither is expressible as an \texttt{enum}. And on the channel a
schema reaches, Tier~A's observance is not observable from outside, while
Tier~B is the same check where we run it, pinned by 36 mutation tests and the
property-based falsifier of Section~\ref{sec:instruments}.

\subsection{The other providers fail at nothing}\label{sec:rq3-second}

On \texttt{gpt-5.4-mini} (same evidence, prompts, scorer and budget, zero
transport errors) all seven arms that existed then show no violation in 200,
the floor included: it cites exactly the ten evidence columns in 199 of 200
responses and never writes \texttt{r2} into a column field. A local
\texttt{qwen2.5:7b} ($N=20$) shows none except \texttt{A-STRICT-STATIC} at
10\,\% (2/20). No comparison between arms exists on those providers, so the
comparative claims of this section rest on one model, and its rates are
properties of \texttt{deepseek-chat} on a date rather than of LLMs. An engineer
who benchmarked prompt-level reliability on \texttt{gpt-5.4-mini} would
conclude the problem does not exist, and would be right about that model on
that day.

\paragraph{The rate moves eightfold, and specification explains part of it}
Six rule-free variants of the bare instruction, over identical evidence and
schema, move the blended entity rate from 9.0\,\% to 72.0\,\%. That wording
moves behaviour this much is established~\cite{sclar2024formatspread}. The
variants are not purely stylistic, though: they differ in how much of the
answer they specify, and that ordering predicts the unverifiable channel
exactly --- 0 and 1 per hundred where the instruction names the statistic, 8
and 10 where it mentions correlations, 26 where it names the field without
saying what goes in it, and 45 where it says nothing. On the entity channel,
the baseline instruction's ``list every column from the evidence you cite''
is the one that produces pure misfiling; Section~\ref{sec:rq1} shows that
describing the field removes it.

\paragraph{Invention of a column is rare, and mostly not invention}
Of the 20 names in 600 responses that $E$ does not carry, 18 are
\texttt{target}: a real column of the frame that $E$ omits, so out-of-evidence
without being non-existent. Two are genuine inventions, both from
\emph{expert}. Misquotation of a carried number occurs once. The sub-channels
show no detectable co-variation across variants (Spearman $\rho=-0.32$,
$p=0.54$, $n=6$); \emph{terse} has the lowest blended rate and the most
out-of-evidence names, but 7 against \emph{mechanical}'s 1 is not significant
($p=0.065$), and an earlier version of this section read too much into it.

\paragraph{Interpretation}
Measured compliance is a property of a configuration: the wording, what the
schema says about each field, the names in the data, the provider and the
date. The rule-bearing prompt removes the failure entirely on this model
(\texttt{A-L2}, Section~\ref{sec:rq3}), and a field description removes the
misfiling (Section~\ref{sec:rq1}); both are strong, cheap and worth using. What
they cannot do is carry a guarantee to the next configuration, because their
effect is only known where it was measured. Two diagnostic arms make the same
point from inside the contract. The evidence-bound enum alone, with no
verification, left no violation in 200 (\texttt{A-TIER-A-ONLY}), and the full
contract under the variant that fails most unenforced produced no violation
on any first attempt: steering at its strongest. Tier~B is the part whose
effect does not have to be re-measured, because it runs in our process over
the returned payload.

\section{RQ5: Does the Contract Transpose?}\label{sec:rq6}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The narrated artifact is \texttt{analyze\_dataset}, a deterministic profiler
shipping in the same tool: per-column type, missing counts and rates,
descriptive statistics, cardinality, outlier counts, and a task-level class
balance. It is bound through the contract layer of Section~\ref{sec:generic}
with no branch naming either task. The shape differs from leakage: the entity
domain is every column of the frame (24 against 10), the statistic domain has
13 names against 1 and depends on the column, and $E$ carries 196 measured
quantities against 10. The evidence comes from running the profiler on a frame
with ordinary data-quality problems --- mixed types, missingness rising across
the categorical block, injected outliers. That frame named its columns
\texttt{num\_feature\_$k$} and \texttt{cat\_feature\_$k$}; after review we
re-ran the unenforced arms on the same data under distinct natural names
(\texttt{age}, \texttt{region}, \ldots), sharing no suffix.

\paragraph{The wrong numbers were our naming}
On the suffixed frame the unconstrained arm put a wrong number in 9 of 200
responses (4.5\,\% [2.4, 8.3]); 11 of its 12 wrong numbers are a real
measurement of the column sharing a suffix --- \texttt{cat\_feature\_07}'s
missing rate on \texttt{num\_feature\_07} --- and one appears nowhere in $E$.
Under distinct names the same model, on the same data, puts a wrong number in
\textbf{none of 400} unconstrained responses. The failure the original frame
measured was produced by names our generator chose, which is what the Devil's
Advocate review suspected.

\paragraph{What remains is real quantities in slots the binder does not admit}
Every other value violation, on both frames, quotes a number $E$ really
carries, and quotes it correctly. Most are the class balance: the profiler
reports it at task level, the narrator attaches it to the target column as
\texttt{class\_balance}, and the binder admits only per-column statistics, so a
correct figure has no admissible slot. The first version of this section
called these an invented quantity kind because we believed the profiler did
not compute one; it does, and we count that as our thirteenth instrument fault
(Section~\ref{sec:instruments}). By Definition~\ref{def:contract} such a claim
is misfiled --- a real item where it is not admissible --- and the verifier
rejects it. For a reader it is harmless and for a consumer of the claim slot it
is still wrong; that the domain excludes it is a design choice of ours that a
richer binding would reverse.

\paragraph{The tiers separate on this task}
Binding the enum removes misplaced claims almost entirely (26 to 4 under
natural names) but not wrong numbers (9 against 11 under suffixed ones): an
enum can refuse a pair outside the admissible statistics and cannot constrain a
number, whose admissible set is a predicate over a continuum parameterised by a
sibling field. Tier~B closes both. \texttt{P-CONTRACT} returned no violation
and sent back 6 responses; \texttt{P-STRESS}, with the enum removed, sent back
13. A re-run of \texttt{P-CONTRACT} with every attempt recorded (200
responses, 8 catches) found no entity violation on any first attempt, so the
enum held on 399 of 400 first attempts; the one exception stays unexplained.
It also caught \texttt{cat\_feature\_03.iqr\_count}, whose column and statistic
are each admissible but whose pair $E$ does not carry --- a relation no
per-field enum expresses and (C2)'s domain check does.

\paragraph{What this establishes}
The contract binds to a second evidence shape without task-specific
verification, and the two tiers separate once the failing channel is one a
schema cannot express. It does not establish that misfiling of numbers is a
property of narrators: on this model it followed our naming, and what survived
distinct names is a binding choice as much as a model error. Two tabular
summaries emitted by one tool are two members of the class, not the class.

Three carry lessons the others repeat. \textbf{A scorer can report a narrator
wrong for being right} (2, 12, 13): comparing every claim with its column's
correlation whatever statistic it named, comparing \texttt{abs\_corr} with a
signed value, and calling a class balance invented when the profiler reports
one all penalised correct claims; the second was found because two readers
disagreed about what a channel measured. \textbf{A control that silently
receives the treatment produces the number the hypothesis wants} (9): the
profile floor ran the deletion step unconditionally and read 0\,\% on a task
that fails, and the tell was sixty Tier~B catches printed for an arm with no
Tier~B. \textbf{Corrections do not run one way by accident}: the first four
that moved the downstream checklist moved it toward our hypothesis, because
false positives concentrate in careful code and the treatment arms write more
of it; the two that broke the pattern came from procedures built to look for
it --- a second scorer written on a different technique, which agreed with the
first on 856 of 858 rule instances before fault 6 was fixed and on all 858
after, and an arm whose adverse outcome was written down before it ran.

Review also found defects in the artifact itself, which the table does not
count because they are not instruments. A claim value of NaN passed the
verifier, because every comparison with NaN is false; the property-based
falsifier the analysis plan registered, built only when review asked for it,
reproduces that refutation at its third generated case on the pre-review code
and then found a second defect --- a 400-digit integer, which a JSON parser
returns as an exact integer, crashed the value check. Both are fixed, and the
fixed verifier passes $10^6$ generated cases. No recorded response carries
either value, so no reported number moves.
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\paragraph{Every failure we measured has a cheap fix, and none carries over}
The misfiling of Section~\ref{sec:rq1} disappears with a rule-bearing prompt
(0\,\%) or one sentence of field description (0.5\,\%); the wrong numbers of
Section~\ref{sec:rq6} disappear under distinct column names; the whole
failure disappears on another provider. Each reappears where its fix is
absent --- 61\,\% on a differently named instance, 49\,\% on the same model
nine days later. A tool author controls the prompt and the schema, but not
the names in the user's data, the provider behind an endpoint, or the date,
and cannot know that every field is specified well enough for every model
without measuring each. That is the argument for Tier~B, and it is an argument
about certification, not about rates: a prompt or a description may work
perfectly, and its effect is known only where it was measured, whereas the
verifier's is a property of code that runs in our process.

\paragraph{What binding at call time buys}
The crowded instance answers this. An enum declared while authoring against
one dataset turned a 61\,\% misfiling rate into a 72.5\,\% invention rate on
the next and blocked the anchor in two responses in three; the same enum
built at call time read zero. A stale schema is not a weaker guarantee but a
source of error, and nothing in a static schema tells its author that the
data has moved.

\paragraph{Simpler designs}
Two alternatives deserve naming. Rendering every structured fact
deterministically and letting the model write prose alone makes the
structured guarantee trivial and leaves the model only the channel no check
reaches. Stripping without retry is cheaper, but would have deleted content
from 6--16\,\% of \texttt{A-STRESS} responses and about 42\,\% of the
Guardrails arms' responses, all of which a single named retry repaired
(Table~\ref{tab:retry}).

\paragraph{A downstream benchmark this paper does not report}
A pre-registered benchmark asked whether handing a model the tool's
deterministic findings reduces six checklist defects in the training scripts
it writes (175 scored runs, four models, three datasets). Findings reduced
flagged defects from 31 to 8, but an arm told only the names of the six scored
rules reached 1, with scripts that executed 47\,\% of the time against
78--83\,\% elsewhere, and held-out scores showed no consistent improvement. We
make no claim from it; its protocol, amendments, runs and both scorer
implementations are in the replication package.

\paragraph{Where the contract transposes}
It needs a deterministic producer, a finite set of observed entities and
values, and a runtime domain bound to that set with independent verification.
Narrators of static-analysis findings, CI failures or dependency-scanner
reports fit; the guarantee there would be consistency with the tool's output,
not that the tool is right.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Threats to Validity}\label{sec:threats}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\paragraph{Construct} The guaranteed property is referential and value
soundness on the structured channels; the verdict, the prose and $E$'s own
correctness are outside it (Section~\ref{sec:contract}). The free text could
in principle absorb what enforcement removes from the structured fields. We
re-ran all eight leakage arms with the narration recorded (1{,}600 responses;
the field had been dropped by the record normaliser, fault 8) and checked
every number in each narration against every quantity in $E$ and every
identifier against every name. Arms that can reject a claim carry ungrounded
content in 10.4\,\% [8.4, 12.7] of narrations, arms that cannot in 9.6\,\%
[7.8, 11.9]; responses that went through a retry read 10.1\,\% against
10.5\,\%. The residue is true bounds and derivations and shortened real
names. \textbf{Displacement is not observed at the scale this instrument sees},
and the instrument is blind in one direction: a misfiled name is in $E$ and so
counts as grounded in prose, so misfiling displaced into the narration would
not register. No enforced arm stripped anything, so the pressure tested is
correction, not deletion; unsupported causal prose needs human raters.

\paragraph{Internal} Arms are compared within one date: the provider serves
a rolling model name, and the unconstrained rates move between runs ---
\texttt{A-GR-STOCK} read 35.0, 43.5 and 47.5\,\% over nine days
(heterogeneity $p=0.035$; pooled 42.0\,\% [38.1, 46.0]), and \texttt{A-STRESS}
repaired 12, 23 and 32 responses ($p=0.006$). The harness and adapter also
changed between runs (the provenance of every record pins both), so a
cross-date difference is not attributable to the provider alone.
\texttt{A-L1} did not move ($p=0.34$).

\paragraph{External} The failure occurs on one of three providers, so every
comparative claim is single-provider and every rate is conditioned on
\texttt{deepseek-chat}, $T=1.0$ and its date. Both leakage instances are
synthetic, and the profile frame is synthetic data under two naming schemes.
The shipped default narrator model was not measured. Schema cost is measured
--- from 10 to 1{,}000 columns the enum costs 38 bytes per column against 344
for the evidence that produced it --- but provider limits on enum size are not,
and the corrective retry inlines the whole list, so its cost grows with width.

\paragraph{Conclusion validity} At $N=200$ a comparison against a zero arm
reaches $p<0.05$ at six events, so the design resolves differences of about
4 percentage points (power 0.8) and cannot separate mechanisms closer than
that. Only the registered family is multiplicity-corrected
(Table~\ref{tab:registered}); every other interval is descriptive.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

For narration whose citable entities and checkable quantities are finite and
enumerable at call time, referential and value soundness on the structured
channels can be enforced in host code behind an API that exposes no logits,
by binding each field's admissible set to the evidence and checking the
returned payload. The mechanism is ordinary validate-and-reask; what this
study adds is what it is up against and what it costs. On the one model that
fails, the failure is the evidence's own vocabulary misfiled into an
under-specified field, not invention; a rule-bearing prompt, a field
description, distinct names or another provider each remove it, and it
returns wherever they are absent. A verifier holds regardless, repaired every
violation it met with one named retry, and cost about half a second. Thirteen
faults in our own instruments and two defects in the verifier, found by
readers and by a falsifier we had registered and not built, are reported with
what each moved. Exporting the check across a tool-server boundary, so that a
client model reading the same evidence is held to it, remains the gap between
what this paper guarantees and what a user of the tool receives.
