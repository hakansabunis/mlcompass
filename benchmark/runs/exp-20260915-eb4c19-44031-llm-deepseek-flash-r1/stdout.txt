🔧 Feature engineering
  • Latitude → Build geo features jointly with Longitude: KMeans clusters 
(k≈10-30) on (Latitude, Longitude), distance to coastline, distance to 
LA/SF/San Diego/San Jose centroids, and a k-nearest-neighbour mean of 'price' 
computed out-of-fold.
     Latitude and Longitude are the only spatial columns (no outliers, ranges 
32.5-42.0 and -124.4 to -114.3) and location is the dominant driver of 
California house values, yet raw coordinates force trees to approximate 
diagonal splits.
  • Longitude → Add rotated coordinates (e.g. rotate lat/lon by ~30-45 degrees)
and their interaction with MedInc.
     The California coastline runs diagonally; rotated axes align with the 
geography and let linear models in the baseline capture the same coastal 
gradient the trees find.
  • AveRooms → Winsorize or log-transform, and derive rooms_per_person = 
AveRooms / AveOccup and bedroom_ratio = AveBedrms / AveRooms.
     AveRooms has IQR outliers (511 rows) and a max of 141.9 versus a median of
5.23 — these are small-denominator artifacts, and the room ratios are the 
standard corrected form of this feature pair.
  • AveBedrms → Winsorize at the 99th percentile (or log1p) before use in the 
linear baseline; keep raw for trees.
     AveBedrms shows the most IQR outliers of any column (1424 rows) with max 
34.07 against a median of 1.05, so it will dominate any unregularized linear 
fit.
  • AveOccup → Log-transform or cap, and replace/augment with households = 
Population / AveOccup.
     AveOccup is extremely right-skewed (mean 3.07, max 1243.3, std 10.4, 711 
IQR outliers) while only 8 rows exceed 3 z-scores; the households count is the 
physically meaningful quantity underneath.
  • Population → Use log1p(Population) and Population / AveOccup; consider 
Population relative to the geographic-neighbourhood mean as a density proxy.
     Population spans 3 to 35,682 with 1196 IQR outliers, and no area column 
exists, so density must be approximated from neighbours or from households.
  • MedInc → Add log(MedInc), MedInc × AveRooms, and MedInc relative to the KNN
neighbourhood mean.
     MedInc has 681 IQR outliers and a max of 15.0 against a median of 3.53, 
and income is usually the single strongest non-spatial predictor — interactions
with the geo clusters localize its effect.
  • HouseAge → Bin into coarse age bands and interact with the geo cluster; add
age × MedInc.
     HouseAge is the cleanest column (0 outliers, range 1-52, median 29) so it 
carries little linear signal alone, but renovation/value dynamics are known to 
be location-dependent.

⚠ Pitfalls
  • Target 'price' is capped: 965 rows (4.7%) sit at exactly 1.79176, matching 
the max of the target range (min 0.1398, max 1.7918). This looks censored, not 
a natural mode.
     → Confirm how the value was recorded before treating it as ordinary 
regression. Options: fit a Tobit/censored regression (or two-part model), train
only on uncapped rows and document the ceiling, or explicitly clip predictions 
to [0.1398, 1.7918] and treat near-cap errors as biased rather than 
informative. Whichever you choose, never report a residual at the cap as if it 
were an unbiased error.
  • Spatial autocorrelation: rows are geographic (Latitude/Longitude, 20,640 
block-group-style records). A random train/test split puts near-identical 
neighbouring locations on both sides.
     → Use spatially blocked or grouped cross-validation (cluster on 
Latitude/Longitude, e.g. k-fold over geo clusters) and report the blocked score
as the honest estimate; compute any KNN-target features out-of-fold to avoid 
leakage.
  • Small-denominator artifacts create the extreme values in AveRooms (max 
141.9), AveBedrms (max 34.07) and AveOccup (max 1243.3) — these are ratios, not
measurement errors to delete.
     → Do not drop as 'errors'. Winsorize or log-transform for the linear 
baseline and any distance-based model; let tree models use them raw and verify 
with a capped-vs-uncapped comparison.
  • Scale mismatch between feature types: Population is in the thousands while 
AveBedrms is ~1, and the target 'price' is on a compressed ~0.14-1.79 scale 
(looks pre-scaled).
     → Fit a StandardScaler/RobustScaler inside the CV pipeline for the linear 
and KNN models (tree models are invariant); if you invert the target scaling 
for reporting, keep the inverse transform inside the same pipeline to avoid 
inconsistency.
  • No missing values are reported in any of the 9 columns, which is unusual 
for real housing data and suggests the file was already cleaned or imputed 
upstream.
     → Confirm the provenance of the file before assuming missingness is a 
non-issue; if imputation was applied, verify it did not use the target or leak 
across the train/test boundary.
  • Potential duplicate or near-duplicate rows (block groups sharing identical 
Latitude/Longitude coordinates).
     → Check for exact duplicates on the coordinate pair plus Population before
splitting; de-duplicate across folds or group them so the same location cannot 
appear in both train and test.
