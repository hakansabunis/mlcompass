🔧 Feature engineering
  • MedInc → consider log-transforming or winsorizing extreme values; 
optionally add bins/quantiles
     It has a wide range and outliers (IQR/z-score counts) in 'MedInc', so a 
monotonic transform can stabilize the scale.
  • AveRooms, AveBedrms, AveOccup, Population → create derived ratios such as 
rooms_per_household proxy features if household count is available; otherwise 
add AveRooms/AveBedrms, bedrooms_per_room, population_per_room, and log1p 
transforms for the heavy-tailed columns
     These columns show strong skew/outliers, and ratio features often capture 
housing density/quality better than raw averages.
  • Latitude + Longitude → engineer spatial features: grid/geohash clusters, 
polynomial/interactions (lat^2, lon^2, lat*lon), or distance-to-coast / 
regional cluster encodings if external geography is allowed
     The target is California housing 'price', so location is likely a major 
driver and simple raw coordinates may miss localized effects.
  • HouseAge → try nonlinear encodings such as splines or age bins 
(newer/mid/older/very old)
     'HouseAge' is bounded (1 to 52) and may relate to price nonlinearly rather
than linearly.
  • All numeric predictors → standardize for linear models; for tree models, 
keep raw and add transformed versions selectively
     The numeric scales differ substantially across 'Population', 'Longitude', 
and 'MedInc', which matters for linear/regularized models.

⚠ Pitfalls
  • Target censoring/capping at the maximum value
     → Investigate why 'price' has 965 rows exactly at 1.79176; if it is a cap,
consider censored-regression methods, separate modeling for capped vs. uncapped
cases, or at minimum evaluate errors specifically near the ceiling.
  • Heavy-tailed predictors and extreme outliers
     → Inspect and possibly cap or transform 'AveRooms', 'AveBedrms', 
'Population', and 'AveOccup'; compare robust scaling/log transforms against raw
features.
  • Potential leakage from inappropriate preprocessing
     → Fit all transforms, scaling, and outlier thresholds only on training 
folds, not the full dataset, to avoid optimistic validation on the 20,640-row 
dataset.
  • Spatial autocorrelation in California housing data
     → Use spatially aware validation if possible, or at least inspect 
residuals by geographic region; random splits can overstate generalization when
nearby tracts appear in both train and test.
  • Multicollinearity among density-related predictors
     → Check correlations among 'AveRooms', 'AveBedrms', 'Population', and 
'AveOccup'; regularization for linear models or feature selection can improve 
stability.
