🔧 Feature engineering
  • Latitude,Longitude → Create `distance_to_coast` (e.g., via Haversine 
formula) or `region_cluster` (K-means on lat/lon).
     Geospatial proximity to coastlines or regional clusters (e.g., urban vs. 
rural) likely correlate with `price` given the dataset's California scope.
  • AveRooms,AveBedrms → Derive `rooms_per_bedroom` (ratio) and `log(AveRooms)`
to reduce skewness and capture relative efficiency.
     High outliers in `AveRooms` (e.g., 141.9) suggest potential data errors; 
log-transforms stabilize variance, while ratios reveal structural 
relationships.
  • Population → Bin into `population_quartiles` or use `log1p(Population)` to 
mitigate extreme skew.
     Population ranges from 3 to 35,682; log-transforms or binning can better 
capture density effects on `price`.
  • price → Treat as censored data: split into `price_below_cap` (≤1.79) and 
`price_cap` (1.79) with a separate model or Tobit regression.
     Warning indicates 4.7% of observations are capped; ignoring this may bias 
predictions near the cap.

⚠ Pitfalls
  • Censored target (`price` capped at 1.79176)
     → Use Tobit regression or split into uncapped/capped subproblems; avoid 
standard regression.
  • Outliers in `AveRooms`/`AveBedrms` (e.g., 141.9 rooms)
     → Investigate outliers (e.g., data entry errors) or cap/winorize extreme 
values before modeling.
  • No explicit train/test split or validation strategy
     → Reserve 20% for validation; use cross-validation to account for small 
regional clusters in lat/lon.
  • No target encoding or feature interactions tested
     → Test interactions like `Latitude*Population` or embed lat/lon as 
categorical regions.
