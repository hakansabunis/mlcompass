such as low/medium/high donation amount.
     V3 has the largest spread and strong right-tail behavior in the summary 
(large max and many outliers), which often benefits from compression or

  • V4 → Use as-is, but also test polynomial or spline-style effects if using 
linear models.
     V4 is numeric with no flagged outliers, so it may carry a smoother 
relationship to Class than the other variables.
  • V1 + V2 + V3 + V4 → Add pairwise interaction terms, especially V1×V3 and 
V2×V3, or let tree-based models learn these interactions directly.
     All predictors are numeric and likely describe related blood-donation 
history variables; interactions can capture behavior not visible in single 
features.

⚠ Pitfalls
  • High duplicate rate (215 exact duplicate rows, 28.7% of the dataset)
     → Verify whether duplicates are genuine repeated records; if not, 
deduplicate before training. If they represent repeated observations, use a 
grouped or time-aware split so copies do not appear in both train and test.
  • Risk of data leakage from random splitting with duplicates
     → Use deduplication or group-based cross-validation/splitting keyed by the
repeated entity to prevent the same record from leaking across folds.
  • Class imbalance (Class 1 about 76.2%, Class 2 about 23.8%)
     → Use stratified splits and imbalance-aware metrics such as F1, balanced 
accuracy, PR-AUC, or ROC-AUC; consider class weights or threshold tuning.
  • Small dataset size after accounting for duplicates
     → Prefer cross-validation over a single holdout split, keep models simple 
initially, and regularize carefully to reduce variance.
  • Outliers in V1, V2, and especially V3
     → Use robust preprocessing, inspect extreme values, and test models that 
are less sensitive to scaling and outliers; for linear models, consider 
transformations or robust scaling.
