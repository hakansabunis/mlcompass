🔧 Feature engineering
  • Latitude, Longitude → Create spatial features such as latitude/longitude 
bins, distance to coast or major metro proxies if available, or cluster-based 
region labels.
     The target is housing-related and location variables are present; 
geographic structure is often highly predictive beyond raw coordinates.
  • AveRooms, AveBedrms → Engineer ratios such as bedrooms_per_room, 
rooms_per_household proxy if a household count exists elsewhere, and 
avg_bedrooms_share = AveBedrms / AveRooms.
     These two columns are related by construction and the raw levels include 
extreme outliers; ratio features often capture housing density and composition 
better.
  • Population, AveOccup → Apply log1p transforms and/or cap extreme values; 
consider interaction terms like Population × AveOccup or population_density 
proxies.
     Both columns show heavy skew/outliers, especially AveOccup and Population,
so transformed versions are likely more informative and stable.
  • MedInc → Add nonlinear transforms such as log(MedInc) and quantile/bin 
indicators.
     MedInc is usually a strong predictor in California housing-style data and 
appears skewed with notable outliers.
  • HouseAge → Try spline, bucketed, or quadratic features for age bands.
     HouseAge has a bounded range (1 to 52) and may relate to price nonlinearly
rather than as a simple linear effect.
  • Latitude, Longitude → Add interaction features with MedInc and HouseAge, 
such as MedInc × latitude and MedInc × longitude.
     Price effects often vary by region; the same income level can map 
differently depending on location.

⚠ Pitfalls
  • Capped/censored target at the upper bound
     → Inspect how price was recorded and consider censored-regression 
approaches or at least flag the capped rows; avoid interpreting standard 
regression errors near the cap as normal noise.
  • Severe outliers in several predictors
     → Use robust preprocessing such as log transforms, winsorization, or 
outlier-aware tree models; check whether extreme values are data-entry 
artifacts or legitimate rare cases.
  • Potential leakage from using raw target-derived or post-outcome variables
     → Verify that none of the predictors are computed using information 
unavailable at prediction time and keep a strict train/validation split before 
any feature engineering that uses target statistics.
  • Need to evaluate with scale-appropriate regression metrics and residual 
analysis
     → Use MAE/RMSE and inspect residuals by target level and by geographic 
region, especially near the capped maximum.
  • Possible multicollinearity among housing composition variables
     → Check correlations among MedInc, AveRooms, AveBedrms, Population, and 
AveOccup; regularize linear models or use feature selection if coefficients are
unstable.
