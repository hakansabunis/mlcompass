🔧 Feature engineering
  • Latitude → Add engineered spatial features: rotated coordinates (e.g. 
0.89*Lat + 0.45*Lon and -0.45*Lat + 0.89*Lon), KMeans cluster ID on (Latitude, 
Longitude), and distance to major CA metros (LA, SF, San Diego, San Jose).
     'Latitude' and 'Longitude' have no outliers and tight ranges (32.54-41.95,
-124.35 to -114.31), so location is clearly a clean, high-signal predictor; raw
coordinates force trees to approximate distance/coastline with axis-aligned 
splits, while explicit distance and cluster features encode it directly.
  • Longitude → Pair with 'Latitude' in interaction terms and in a KNN-distance
feature (mean target or mean MedInc of the k nearest blocks) computed inside 
cross-validation folds only.
     Coastal vs inland price gradients in California are diagonal, not 
axis-aligned; a target-encoding of location computed out-of-fold captures that 
gradient that neither 'Longitude' alone nor a deep single tree splits well.
  • MedInc → log1p(MedInc), plus interactions MedInc*Latitude and 
MedInc*Longitude, and a rank/quantile transform.
     'MedInc' is right-skewed with q50 3.53 but max 15.0001 and 681 IQR 
outliers, and it is usually the single strongest predictor of house value - a 
log transform and spatial interactions let a linear baseline compete.
  • AveRooms → Create ratios: AveRooms/AveOccup (rooms per person), 
AveBedrms/AveRooms (bedroom share), and Population/AveOccup (estimated 
households); apply log1p to AveRooms itself.
     'AveRooms' has 511 IQR outliers and a max of 141.9 against a q50 of 5.23, 
so raw values are dominated by a handful of tiny-household blocks; ratios 
normalize this and expose household structure.
  • AveOccup → log1p transform plus winsorization/clipping at a high 
percentile, and an explicit flag for AveOccup above ~10.
     'AveOccup' has q75 3.28 but a max of 1243, which almost certainly reflects
group quarters or a data error rather than a real household; an indicator lets 
the model separate the anomaly from the continuous effect.
  • Population → log1p(Population) and derived Population/AveOccup household 
count; drop or flag the extreme tail.
     'Population' has 1,196 IQR outliers and a max of 35,682 versus q50 1,166, 
so the raw variable is long-tailed and a log transform stabilizes its influence
on any distance- or coefficient-based model.
  • HouseAge → Keep as-is; optionally add a binning (deciles) or a flag for 
HouseAge >= 52, and an interaction with the location clusters.
     'HouseAge' shows zero IQR and zero z-score outliers and a max of 52 - the 
fact that it does not exceed 52 suggests it may be capped, so an age-cap flag 
is safer than treating the top value as literal.
  • price → Create a censoring indicator for price == 1.79176 and consider 
modeling it jointly (e.g. two-stage: P(capped) then regression on uncapped 
rows).
     965 rows (4.7%) sit exactly at the target maximum 1.79176, so the target 
is effectively censored and a single-regression fit will be systematically 
biased at that boundary.

⚠ Pitfalls
  • Target 'price' is piled up at its maximum 1.79176 (965 rows, 4.7%), 
indicating a censored/capped measurement.
     → Fit a censored (Tobit-style) or quantile/robust-loss model, or drop/flag
capped rows for evaluation; never report or trust predictions above 1.79176, 
and inspect residuals specifically near the cap.
  • Extreme outliers in 'AveRooms' (max 141.9), 'AveBedrms' (1,424 IQR 
outliers, max 34.1), 'AveOccup' (max 1243) and 'Population' (max 35,682) that 
may be genuine group housing or data errors.
     → Log-transform and/or winsorize these columns, add anomaly flags, and 
verify a sample of extreme rows against the source; prefer tree models or 
robust scaling so a few rows cannot dominate the loss.
  • 'MedInc' (max 15.0001) and 'HouseAge' (max 52) both stop at suspiciously 
round upper values, suggesting they are also top-coded.
     → Treat the top codes as censored: add top-code indicator features rather 
than letting the model read them as exact magnitudes.
  • Strong spatial autocorrelation: neighboring census blocks have nearly 
identical 'Latitude'/'Longitude' and therefore near-identical prices.
     → Use spatially blocked or grouped cross-validation (e.g. group by 
location cluster) instead of a random split, and compute any location-based 
target encoding strictly out-of-fold to avoid leakage.
  • No missingness is reported, which may mean missing values were silently 
imputed or that rows with missing fields were dropped before this file was 
produced.
     → Confirm the provenance of the CSV, check for duplicated rows and for 
physically implausible values (e.g. AveOccup of 1243), and document the 
cleaning steps as part of the pipeline.
  • Target range is narrow (0.14-1.79, std 0.36) and bounded, so plain OLS can 
predict values outside the observed range and near the cap.
     → Clip predictions to the observed target range for reporting, and prefer 
models with bounded or robust loss when the metric is sensitive to tail errors.
