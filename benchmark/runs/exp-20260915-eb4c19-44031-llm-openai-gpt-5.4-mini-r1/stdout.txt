🔧 Feature engineering
  • Latitude, Longitude → Create spatial features such as latitude/longitude 
bins, interaction terms, distance-to-coast proxies, or clustering-based 
location embeddings.
     The target 'price' is likely strongly location-dependent, and raw 
'Latitude'/'Longitude' may not fully capture geographic structure.
  • MedInc → Try log transform or winsorization and add interaction terms with 
geography (e.g., MedInc × Latitude, MedInc × Longitude).
     MedInc has a wide range and outliers, and income usually interacts 
strongly with location for housing prices.
  • AveRooms, AveBedrms, Population, AveOccup → Engineer ratio features such as
bedrooms-per-room (AveBedrms / AveRooms), rooms-per-person (AveRooms / 
Population), and occupancy-adjusted density features.
     These columns are already averages/count-like measures, so derived ratios 
can better capture household density and housing quality than the raw 
aggregates alone.
  • HouseAge → Add non-linear transforms or binning (e.g., age bands, squared 
term, spline basis).
     HouseAge spans 1 to 52 with no missingness; the relationship to 'price' is
likely non-linear rather than purely linear.
  • All numeric predictors → Standardize features for linear models and 
consider robust scaling or log1p on heavy-tailed variables like Population and 
AveOccup.
     Several predictors show strong skew/outliers (notably 'Population', 
'AveOccup', 'AveBedrms', 'AveRooms'), which can hurt linear methods and 
destabilize optimization.

⚠ Pitfalls
  • Capped/censored target at the maximum value
     → Investigate why 965 rows have 'price' exactly at 1.79176; if it is a 
cap, consider censored-regression ideas, modeling the cap separately, or at 
least evaluate errors near the ceiling carefully.
  • Heavy outliers and extreme skew in several predictors
     → Use robust preprocessing, log transforms, winsorization, or tree-based 
models; inspect whether extreme values in 'AveOccup', 'AveBedrms', and 
'Population' are data errors or legitimate edge cases.
  • Potential leakage-free but non-iid spatial structure
     → Use spatially aware validation splits or at least check performance by 
geographic region, since 'Latitude' and 'Longitude' suggest strong location 
clustering that can make random splits optimistic.
  • Average-based features may hide underlying denominators
     → Verify how 'AveRooms', 'AveBedrms', and 'AveOccup' were constructed and 
whether corresponding totals or household counts are available; ratio features 
can be informative, but only if the definitions are consistent.
