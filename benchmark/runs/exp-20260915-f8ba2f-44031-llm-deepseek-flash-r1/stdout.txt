🔧 Feature engineering
  • Latitude, Longitude → Add rotated coordinates (e.g. 45° rotation), distance
to the Pacific coast, distance to LA/SF/San Diego centroids, and KMeans cluster
IDs or a coarse grid cell.
     Latitude and Longitude are the only geospatial columns and their raw 
linear form forces a monotone effect that cannot represent California's 
coastal/urban price structure.
  • MedInc → Use log1p(MedInc) and add interactions with geo cluster or 
distance-to-coast; also try binning into deciles for the linear baseline.
     MedInc is the strongest classic predictor (mean 3.87, max 15.0, 681 IQR 
outliers) and its effect on price is typically nonlinear and 
location-dependent.
  • AveRooms, AveBedrms → Add the ratio AveRooms/AveBedrms and BedroomsPerRoom 
= AveBedrms/AveRooms; consider clipping or log-transforming both.
     AveBedrms shows the most IQR outliers (1,424) and AveRooms reaches 141.9, 
so their raw values are skewed while the ratio is bounded and more informative.
  • Population, AveOccup → Derive Households = Population / AveOccup, and 
log-transform Population and AveOccup; treat AveOccup values near 1243 as data 
errors or special cases.
     AveOccup has max 1243 in a column whose median is 2.82 — plausible only if
it encodes an unusual recording; Households recovers a bounded, meaningful unit
count.
  • price → Add a binary is_capped flag (price == 1.79176) and consider 
excluding capped rows from the fit or modelling them separately.
     965 rows (4.7%) sit at exactly the maximum 1.79176, so the target is 
censored rather than a natural mode and the cap is not a genuine observation.

⚠ Pitfalls
  • Censored target: 4.7% of 'price' rows (965) are pinned at the maximum 
1.79176, flagged in the dataset warnings.
     → Fit a Tobit / censored regression, or add an is_capped indicator and 
drop or down-weight capped rows; evaluate with MAE so errors near the cap are 
not dominated by squared-loss bias.
  • Heavy-tailed predictors: AveOccup (max 1243), Population (max 35,682), 
AveRooms (max 141.9), AveBedrms (1,424 IQR outliers).
     → Log-transform or winsorize these columns and use scale-robust models 
(tree ensembles) or a RobustScaler/QuantileTransformer for the linear baseline;
do not drop outliers blindly since they may be real geography.
  • Spatial autocorrelation and repeated coordinates: rows are aggregated block
groups, so neighbouring rows are not independent.
     → Use spatially grouped cross-validation (e.g. group by rounded 
Latitude/Longitude or by KMeans cluster) instead of random KFold, and check for
duplicate coordinate rows before splitting.
  • Latitude/Longitude used as plain linear features in a linear model, where 
the true relationship is non-monotone.
     → Feed splines, rotations, or geo-cluster one-hots into the linear 
baseline, or rely on a tree model that can partition space; compare both to 
confirm the geographic signal is captured.
  • Derived-column leakage: AveRooms and AveBedrms already embed household 
counts that overlap with Population/AveOccup.
     → Build the ratio and Households features explicitly, then check pairwise 
correlation and drop redundant variants before fitting the interpretable 
baseline.
