# A/B benchmark: does mlcompass change how an LLM trains a model?

Result report, 2026-09-15. Self-contained: every number below is either in
this document or recomputable from files named in it. Companion to
`REPORT_2026-09-15.md`, which covers the *detection* benchmark (does
mlcompass find known defects in a dataset). This one covers the *A/B*
benchmark, which asks a different question with a different measure.

- Protocol: `ab_protocol.md` (version A/B 1.3, amendments A1–A5 in its §9)
- Harness: `run_ab.py`
- Rows: `ab_results.csv`, 108
- Evidence: `runs/ab-20260915-3b0221-*`, one directory per run

---

## 1. The question and the design

> Given the same dataset and the same model, does an LLM asked to write a
> training script produce better work when mlcompass's findings are in
> front of it than when they are not?

Three arms. Each differs from the one above it in exactly one thing.

| Arm | What it receives |
| --- | --- |
| `control` | CSV path, target column, column list, list of installed packages, the task |
| `advise` | the above **plus** the verbatim output of `mlcompass advise` |
| `advise+audit` | the `advise` arm, **plus** one revision round: its own script is passed through `mlcompass audit` and the findings returned to the same model |

The one-difference property is checked from the evidence rather than
asserted: `build_prompts` returns `(control, control + block)`, so there is
one construction and not two templates that can drift, and every `run.json`
carries both prompt digests. In the first validation pair the control
prompt was 612 characters, the advise prompt 2690, and
`treatment.startswith(control)` was true.

Each arm audits **only its own** script. Feeding one arm's output to
another would make them a single three-step condition rather than three
comparable ones.

### Outcomes, in priority order

| Outcome | Measured by |
| --- | --- |
| `holdout_score` | the script's saved model, loaded by the harness and scored on a holdout the script never sees |
| `process_defects` | a frozen six-item checklist over the emitted source, applied by a script, never by a model |
| `runs_at_all` | the emitted script's exit status |

The six defects: `no_seed`, `leak_fit_before_split`, `leak_duplicate_rows`,
`wrong_metric_for_imbalance`, `no_validation`, `target_in_features`.

### The losing condition, written before any data existed

> If `advise` does not beat `control` on held-out score and does not lower
> the defect count, then being told about the data did not improve the
> work. If `advise+audit` does not beat `advise` on either measure, then
> the revision round did not either. Each is reported in those words for
> the step it concerns.

## 3. The result

### Defects fall

Sum over the 9 runs of each (lane, arm) cell:

| Lane | `control` | `advise` | `advise+audit` |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| deepseek-flash | 14 | 15 | **7** |
| mistral-ministral-8b | 8 | 2 | **0** |
| ollama-qwen2.5-7b | 6 | 4 | **1** |
| openai-gpt-5.4-mini | 12 | 10 | **7** |

Every lane ends with fewer defects than it started. **The first step is not
uniform** — deepseek rises from 14 to 15 before falling to 7 — so the
accurate statement is that the defect count falls from `control` to
`advise+audit` on all four lanes, not that it falls at every step. An
earlier draft of this claim said "on every lane" about both steps and was
wrong; it is corrected here rather than quietly restated.

### The held-out score does not move

Median per (lane, dataset, arm), and the largest within-arm spread across
the three repetitions of that cell:

| Lane | Dataset | `control` | `advise` | `advise+audit` | max within-arm spread |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| deepseek-flash | 1464 | 0.5253 | 0.5255 | 0.5740 | 0.2122 |
| deepseek-flash | 1480 | 0.6797 | 0.6832 | 0.6861 | 0.0512 |
| deepseek-flash | 44031 | 0.8503 | 0.8503 | 0.8509 | 0.0052 |
| ministral-8b | 1464 | 0.5998 | 0.5433 | 0.5365 | 0.0695 |
| ministral-8b | 1480 | 0.6795 | 0.6905 | 0.7025 | 0.0380 |
| ministral-8b | 44031 | 0.8145 | −0.0912 | 0.8170 | 2.2446 |
| qwen2.5-7b | 1464 | 0.5268 | 0.5400 | 0.5400 | 0.0000 |
| qwen2.5-7b | 44031 | 0.8144 | 0.6281 | 0.6281 | 0.1863 |
| gpt-5.4-mini | 1480 | 0.6623 | 0.7083 | 0.6215 | 0.0163 |

Counting `control` → `advise+audit` across the nine cells with both ends
scored: **3 better, 3 worse, 3 within ±0.01.** No direction.

**The spread inside an arm frequently exceeds the gap between arms.**
deepseek's `advise` arm on 1464 spans 0.2122 across three repetitions while
its median sits 0.0002 above control. Reading a 0.0002 difference out of
that spread would be reading noise.

### So: the outcome the protocol named in advance

`ab_protocol.md` §6, written before any run:

> A plausible and publishable outcome is that the scores are
> indistinguishable while the defect count falls. That would say mlcompass
> improves the *process* without improving the *metric*, which is worth
> stating precisely rather than dressing up as either a win or a failure.

That is what happened. It is reported in those words because the words were
chosen before the data existed.

### Where the losing condition was met

Stated per cell rather than averaged away:

- `advise` did **not** improve on `control` for deepseek on 44031, and for
  qwen on 44031.
- The revision round did **not** improve on `advise` for ministral on 1464,
  for qwen on 44031, and on the defect half for gpt on 44031.

No p-value and no significance claim. `ab_protocol.md` §9 records that
"beats" has no decision rule yet, and inventing one after seeing the data is
the failure the pre-registration exists to prevent. With n=3 per cell,
medians separated by 0.0002, and ranges up to 2.24, the absence of a
decision rule is now the binding constraint on what can be reported — not
the amount of data.

## 4. What `mlcompass audit` actually contributed

This needed establishing rather than assuming, because `audit` was silent on
this kind of script until the same day.

**32 of 36** third-arm runs received findings; 4 got "No issues detected".
The revision rewrote the script in 35 of 36 runs and **moved a defect flag in
10**: `no_seed` fixed ×5, `no_validation` fixed ×3, `leak_duplicate_rows`
fixed ×2, and one run introduced `no_seed` while fixing two others.

Which rules fired:

| Rule | Count | Lanes |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `seed` (error) | 21 | deepseek 5, gpt 9, ministral 7 |
| `val_split` (warning) | 18 | deepseek 9, gpt 9 |
| `unused_holdout` (warning) | 8 | qwen 6, ministral 2 |

Of the six sklearn rules added to `audit` earlier the same day, **only
`unused_holdout` contributed**. The other 39 findings came from two
pre-existing rules that now reach these scripts because the models import
numpy (`seed`) or skip the split entirely (`val_split`).

## 5. Two design defects found before the battery, and what they cost

Both were in the A/B protocol, not in the detection protocol Yusuf wrote.

### 5.1 The benchmark was rewarding the leak it was built to penalise

The harness split the pinned CSV directly. OpenML 1464 carries 215 duplicate
rows in 748, so a stratified split at 0.25 put **71 of 187 holdout rows
verbatim into train** — measured, not estimated. A script that de-duplicates
its training data therefore deletes rows that are exact copies of holdout
rows. A script that leaves them in is scored on rows it memorised.

Across twelve validation runs the relationship was inverted and completely
separated:

| `leak_duplicate_rows` | held-out ROC AUC | runs |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| present (the defect) | 0.6532 – 0.6835 | 8 |
| absent (correct) | 0.5552 exactly | 4 |

Nothing between the groups. Run at full scale first, this would have
produced a clean, consistent, entirely wrong result — and the cleanness is
what would have made it convincing.

**Fix (A4):** split on duplicate groups, so every set of identical rows
lands entirely on one side. mlcompass's own warning names both remedies —
de-duplicate before splitting, *or split on a group key* — and the second
was chosen because the first deletes the defect along with the leak. On
1464: train 524, holdout 224, **0 holdout rows present in train, 125
duplicates still inside train**. The score is honest and the defect stays
reachable. Verified on all three datasets.

### 5.2 The prompt did not say what was installed

A validation run's `advise` arm died on `import imblearn` — a reasonable
library, absent here. So `runs_at_all`, one of three outcomes, was partly
measuring our own `pip list`.

The confound is **directional**, which is what made it worth an amendment:
advise output recommends handling class imbalance, which points a model at
exactly the resampling libraries most likely to be missing. Left unstated,
the environment would penalise the treatment arms for taking the
intervention's advice, and the penalty would look like the intervention
making things worse.

**Fix (A5):** every arm receives the same list, resolved from the
interpreter that will actually run the script rather than written by hand.
Result: **zero `ModuleNotFoundError` across all 108 runs.** The 21 remaining
script failures are `ValueError` (unencoded categoricals, shape errors) and
`NameError` — real code defects, not environment artefacts.

One detail worth recording: the list is a curated 36-name roster, not a
`pip list` dump, because this environment holds 171 distributions and one of
them is `mlcompass` itself. A complete dump would have printed the
intervention's own name into the prompt of the arm defined by *not* being
given it.

## 6. One measurement problem, recorded and deliberately not fixed

`SCORER_SOURCE` requires the loaded object to expose `.predict`. **17
completed runs scored blank** — every one of them carrying the note "no
saved artefact could be loaded and used to predict", verified by reading
`ab_results.csv` rather than taken from a summary. The identified causes
are a saved *bundle* (model plus encoder plus column list, which has no
`.predict` of its own) in 15 of them, and in one case a script that
label-encoded the target from {1,2} to {0,1}, after which the scorer
refused the class mismatch. Both are scripts doing arguably the *more*
correct thing and being scored blank for it. The seventeenth is not
individually attributed here; the per-run reason is in its own
`runs/.../scoring.md`.

It hit `gpt-5.4-mini` hardest: **22 of its 27 runs are unscored**, 14 of
them for this reason. **That lane's held-out column should not be read**
until an amendment decides whether a bundle counts.

It is not fixed because the plan was frozen before execution, and changing
a measure after seeing results is precisely the failure a pre-registration
exists to prevent. It is an open amendment (A6), not a bug left lying.

## 8. Limits, stated plainly

- **Three datasets, one task family.** Two binary classification and one
  regression, all tabular. Nothing here generalises beyond that.
- **n=3 per cell.** Enough to show that the within-arm spread swamps the
  between-arm gap; not enough to put an interval on anything.
- **No decision rule for "beats".** Reported as direction of medians with
  spread attached. This is the binding constraint, not the data.
- **One lane is largely unscored.** `gpt-5.4-mini`, for the reason in §6.
- **One seed across the three repetitions**, so the holdout is identical
  everywhere and the spread is generation variance alone. The protocol does
  not say whether repetitions should re-split; this was a choice and it is
  recorded as one.
- **`audit`'s contribution is mostly from two old rules**, not from the six
  added the same day. `§10 L1` of the companion report says `audit` was
  silent on 5 of 5 tabular sklearn scripts; on this battery it spoke in 32
  of 36. That limitation is out of date for this dataset family, and the
  protocol has not been edited to say so after seeing data.

## 9. What this does and does not license

**It licenses:** the statement that across four models and three tabular
datasets, giving an LLM mlcompass's findings reduced the number of process
defects in the training code it wrote, in every lane, while leaving the
held-out score without a consistent direction and with run-to-run variance
larger than the between-arm difference.

**It does not license:** "mlcompass makes models better." The score did not
move. Nor does it license a claim about which step does the work — the
revision round looks stronger than the advice block on three of four lanes,
but one lane rose before falling and one lane's scores are mostly unreadable.

The honest headline is the one §6 named before the data existed: **the
process improves, the metric does not.**
